# Farmer apps and the opportunity for Krishi Queue

**Research focus:** Chhattisgarh, especially paddy procurement, with relevant Indian services for comparison.  
**Research reviewed:** 1 November 2026.  
**Purpose:** Understand which services are already used, what they solve, where farmers may still struggle, and whether Krishi Queue can be useful and commercially sustainable.

This is desk research. It is not a farmer survey, an audit of other apps, a live test of their full workflows, or proof that any reported problem affects all farmers. The [full Chhattisgarh research](RESEARCH_CHHATTISGARH.md) contains the broader policy, rural-access and startup analysis; [implementation status](docs/IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md) explains what Krishi Queue actually does today.

## 1. The answer in plain language

Farmers and procurement systems **already use digital services at meaningful scale**. Chhattisgarh has an official token app. Other services provide market prices, weather, crop advice, farmer registration and procurement information. Some also combine an app with physical shops, field staff or telephone support.

That does not mean every farmer can use them independently, that every booking succeeds, or that a token solves the whole selling journey. A farmer can still face a closed or overloaded centre, a record mismatch, transport costs, a weighing dispute or uncertainty about payment. The evidence supports investigating these gaps; it does not prove how common each gap is across Chhattisgarh.

**The opportunity:** help a farmer complete an *official* sale with fewer avoidable problems: understand what to prepare, check whether a trip is worthwhile, retain evidence, and get a specific problem resolved. The app must work alongside the official system and the people who operate it. An unofficial token cannot reserve a government slot.

**The business reality:** a free farmer-facing tool could be funded by a society, FPO, procurement operator or authorised partner if it demonstrably saves effort or improves outcomes. No paying customer, government access or savings are established yet.

## 2. How to read the evidence

| Measure | What it tells us | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| App downloads | An app was installed at least once, sometimes on shared or replacement devices | Unique farmers, current use or successful bookings |
| Registered farmers | Accounts or farmer records were created | How many people use the service independently or regularly |
| Queries, tokens or transactions | Some activity took place | Unique users, satisfaction, accuracy or whether every transaction completed |
| Farmers who sold to government | Procurement participation | How many booked using an app themselves |
| Reported outages or complaints | Particular people or locations experienced a problem | The percentage of all farmers affected |
| Company-reported reach | The scale a company says it reaches | Independently audited active users or farmer income gains |

Dates and definitions matter. A seasonal procurement app may be used heavily for a few weeks and rarely after harvest; low daily use outside that period is not necessarily failure.

## 4. The most important competitor: Token Tuhar

### What it already does well

The state app lets eligible farmers use an official channel for procurement tokens and related information. NIC describes electronic procurement records and a society-based offline token route. The app's distribution and NIC's usage statement make it unreasonable to describe the market as having no digital solution. [NIC Chhattisgarh profile](https://informatics.nic.in/files/websites/october-2025/chhattisgarh.php)

The government has also described farmers using mobile tokens as a way to reduce trips and queues. Those accounts are useful evidence of intended benefits and reported success, but they are government communications rather than independent measurements of average time saved. [Chhattisgarh public-relations account](https://jansampark.cg.gov.in/dprnewsportal/NewsPopup.aspx?param=GI%2FqlwyHrZk%3D&type=TQwAi5bC7eg%3D)

### Why a farmer may still have difficulty

- A slot can be unavailable because the centre has limited capacity. A faster interface cannot create more storage, workers, bags, scales or transport.
- A slow connection, overloaded server or short booking window may lead to repeated attempts. Reports from particular locations describe such difficulties, but cannot be treated as a statewide failure rate. [Durg reporting](https://english.dainikjagranmpcg.com/states/chhattisgarh/disorder-at-paddy-procurement-centers-in-durg-leaves-farmers-frustrated/article-9401); [Mungeli reporting](https://lalluram.com/server-crash-at-paddy-procurement-centers-leads-to-long-queues-of-farmers-payments-through-micro-atm-are-negligible/)
- A smartphone may be shared, controlled by another household member or unavailable at the exact moment it is needed. A society-assisted route matters, but its local availability and wait must be checked.
- Registration, land, crop or identity records may require correction. A successful phone login is not proof of procurement eligibility.
- A valid token still does not confirm final accepted weight, quality, bill or money received in the bank.

These are **problems to validate**, not a claim that the official app lacks a particular feature or that it fails for most farmers. Its current behaviour, rules and partner permissions need hands-on verification with the department and actual users.

### What could improve the journey

1. Before travel, make the current official date, centre, documents and any known blocker easy to understand.
2. Give an authorised operator a simple way to publish a dated centre update, including when it has not been refreshed. Do not substitute a guess for official capacity.
3. Help farmers keep the official reference and paper or digital receipts, and understand what each receipt proves.
4. When a mismatch, quality concern or payment delay occurs, explain which office to contact, what evidence to bring and how to record the complaint number.
5. Ensure a farmer can use a society desk, a trusted helper or paper without being forced to own a smartphone.

## 5. Why adoption may be limited even when apps exist

**Access is not the same as independent use.** A household may have a smartphone while the farmer has no control over it during the day; internet availability at home does not guarantee usable signal at a centre. National rural averages do not establish access in a particular Chhattisgarh village. The [full research](RESEARCH_CHHATTISGARH.md) discusses household access and gender differences with survey sources.

**Language, literacy and confidence matter.** Technical labels, English-first instructions, small buttons and complex forms can make a service difficult even when it technically supports Hindi. Chhattisgarhi and voice support should be reviewed with local speakers, not assumed correct because a translation API exists.

**The service must solve an urgent job.** A farmer may open a procurement app only during procurement season. Advice that is generic, late or unrelated to their crop and district may not justify mobile data or attention.

**Trust comes from a result and a person.** A farmer may rely on a society worker, local dealer or agent for help with paperwork, credit and transport. A replacement app that removes their support without providing an alternative could make access worse. A Tamil Nadu study of 390 participants found digital literacy, education, social participation and market distance associated with e-NAM participation; it does not establish the size of these effects in Chhattisgarh. [Peer-reviewed study](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJEE/article/view/178205)

**An app cannot fix a broken physical process alone.** If a centre lacks available capacity, calibrated equipment, staff, storage or timely lifting, digital messages can describe the problem but cannot resolve it without an accountable operator.

## 6. What Krishi Queue should offer

### A. Before the visit

- A simple, local-language checklist: society, crop, expected quantity, planned date, documents requested by the society, transport and official reference.
- A prominent distinction between a **personal plan** and a **government-confirmed token**. Never convert a locally generated QR code into a claim of official acceptance.
- Offline access to saved information after installation or the first successful light-page visit. If the connection fails, show what was saved and when; do not promise that an unsent request reserved a place.
- A dated centre update only when a responsible, authorised source can maintain it; show “not updated” when its freshness cannot be established.
- Easy sharing or printing, with an explicit reminder to keep the official token and receipts.

### B. At the centre

- Explain the sequence: arrival, weighing, quality, purchase receipt and payment. Actual procedures and applicable policy must be checked for each season.
- Where permitted and supported by the official system, show measured gross/tare/net weight, quality decision and any deduction with the source and timestamp.
- Offer an understandable path to query a discrepancy or request a recheck. Do not fabricate a completed inspection or make a chatbot's answer an official decision.
- Support self-arranged transport; do not make an app-assigned driver a condition of selling.

### C. After the sale

- Help the farmer retain the purchase receipt, accepted quantity, bill and official payment reference.
- Distinguish “bill created”, “transfer initiated”, “failed/needs correction” and “money confirmed by the authoritative source”. A bill alone does not prove money reached a bank.
- Provide a complaint evidence checklist, responsible office, date, reference and escalation instructions. A real tracking promise requires a named owner and an actual way to resolve cases.

### D. Useful information and assistance

- Provide short Hindi and English offline explanations; commission locally reviewed Chhattisgarhi rather than guessing dialect quality.
- Offer BHASHINI translation/voice only when approved access and the actual feature are available; show when a connection is needed and allow the farmer to keep the original.
- Use an offline topic-based helper for common questions and optional consent-based online help. Never ask for OTP, PIN, Aadhaar or bank details in chat; refer crop diagnosis or chemical dosing to qualified local experts.
- Show mandi prices with their market, date, grade and source. Prices are historical reports, not a guaranteed offer or necessarily the procurement rate.

## 7. Design for 2G and rural access

| Situation | Farmer-friendly response | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|
| No data when opening an installed app | Open saved plans and bundled help immediately | Live status cannot be refreshed without a connection |
| First visit on a slow browser connection | Offer a small text-first light page before downloading the full app | First download still needs internet; browser storage may later be cleared |
| Request interrupted | Preserve the farmer's draft and show whether anything was actually accepted | Do not call a draft “confirmed”; real submissions require server acknowledgement and safe retries |
| Shared phone | Ask before sharing, limit stored personal data, make sign-out and deletion clear | Local separation is not a substitute for an encrypted official system |
| No smartphone, limited literacy or no signal | Assisted society/FPO desk, phone support, paper reference and locally appropriate communication | These channels need staff, training and operating funds; software alone does not create them |
| Stale reports | Display observation date and last successful update | A recent download of an old price does not make the price current |

Krishi Queue's light browser shell was tested at simulated 64 kbit/s and consists of approximately 23 KB of core files before compression. This is a controlled test, not proof that every rural device or network will work. The full installed Android app and official services have different download and connectivity needs. See [implementation status](docs/IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md).

## 8. What is implemented today, and what is not

| Area | Present in the project | Still required |
|---|---|---|
| Farmer access | Guest mode, core Hindi/English screens, larger text, offline help and a saved personal visit planner | Farmer usability testing, independently reviewed Chhattisgarhi, accessible voice and real assisted delivery |
| Light access | Separate small browser version and offline shell after first successful caching | Field tests on real low-end devices and intermittent networks; light and Flutter plans do not synchronise |
| Sign-in | Google/OTP integration code and claims-based roles | Live Firebase provider setup, OAuth credentials/fingerprints, testing on actual platforms |
| BHASHINI and online help | Authenticated gateway implementation and consent-based text-translation flow | Provider credentials, authorised production access, deployment and live tests; voice is not implemented |
| Prices/news | Source-specific parsing, dates, manual refresh and cached reports | Configured/deployed provider gateway; independent verification of the newest records |
| Government procurement | Clearly marked practice workflow and invalid-for-procurement sample QR | An authorised partner, real verification, official tokens, capacity, weighing, receipts and payment integration |
| Cases and support | Practical guidance on evidence and where to seek help | Staffed operation, real case submission, ownership, escalation, service levels and audit |

Nothing has been deployed to government systems. Offline personal notes are not an official reservation, a cloud backup or a promise that an action will sync later.

## 9. Could the developers earn from this?

Yes, **if an organisation finds measurable value and agrees to pay**. This is a hypothesis, not current revenue or a guaranteed market.

| Possible payer | What they could pay for | Evidence needed first |
|---|---|---|
| Procurement society or operator | Training, setup, support, an authorised readiness/case workflow | Permission to use the system, less duplicate work and an operator able to maintain updates |
| FPO or cooperative | Member assistance, transport coordination and accessible information | Members actually use it and the group can support it without excluding non-members |
| Government/authorised implementation partner | Pilot delivery, integration, operations and support under an approved contract | Procurement eligibility, security review, clear scope and alignment with existing systems |
| Funded agricultural programme | A time-limited assisted-access or research pilot | Agreed outcomes, ethical consent, reporting and a plan after the grant ends |

Keep core farmer help free if possible. Avoid charging farmers for access to government benefits, selling personal information, or giving paid suppliers preferential advice without clear disclosure. Check the partner's procurement rules and applicable law before signing contracts.

Estimate a seasonal price from **setup + staff training + hosting + support hours + SMS/provider use + maintenance + contingency + a reasonable margin**. Subtract any grant or partner contribution only if confirmed. Cloud functions, SMS, online AI and BHASHINI production access can have costs; free development allowances are not a promise of free production. Set a budget and measure actual usage during a pilot.

The biggest commercial risks are lack of authorised data access, overlap with UPAHAR or another incumbent, staff being asked to enter the same information twice, and a farmer-facing service that promises a resolution nobody is responsible for delivering.

## 10. A practical research and launch plan

1. **Speak to users and operators.** With consent, observe farmers, society staff, helpers and women using the current process. Include people who did not successfully use an app, not just confident smartphone users.
2. **Map a real season and centre.** Record the official sequence, policy version, booking channel, capacity constraints, document checks, weighing, payment and complaint ownership. Confirm current rules with the responsible authority.
3. **Choose one measurable problem.** For example, failed journeys due to an uncommunicated closure or unresolved record mismatches. Agree what the partner can actually change.
4. **Establish a baseline.** Record journeys, time, transport costs, failed attempts, resolution and staff workload before the pilot. Protect personal information and do not require sensitive identifiers for research unnecessarily.
5. **Run a small assisted pilot.** Compare with a suitable existing-process group or before/after baseline while accounting for weather, harvest and staffing differences. Include paper and human support.
6. **Measure outcomes, not just installs.** Track successful sales, avoidable trips, total waiting, complaint resolution, payment evidence, staff effort and whether small/marginal farmers or women were left behind.
7. **Decide whether to expand.** Continue only if data is trustworthy, staff can maintain the service, access is authorised, costs are sustainable and benefits persist. If not, narrow the product to offline preparation and assisted guidance.

The recommended position is therefore **farmer assistance around an authorised procurement system**, proved at one centre before claiming a statewide solution.
